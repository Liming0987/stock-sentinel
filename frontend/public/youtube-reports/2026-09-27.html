Target's Management Team Deserves Credit for Accelerating Revenue Growth Amid a Challenging Backdrop
2026-09-26 · 6m
Parkev credits Target's new management team for reinvigorating growth after a long slump, with net sales up 5.3% in the latest quarter, traffic up nearly 4%, and full-year guidance raised to roughly 5% growth for 2026. He notes sales had been declining from a 2023 peak until early 2026, and trailing revenue now totals about $108 billion, up from around $70 billion in 2017. He praises Target as an early leader in the digital transformation — buy-online-pickup-in-store and drive-up delivery — a playbook Walmart, Home Depot and others later copied.

He frames Target as a more premium retailer that struggled as pinched consumer budgets pushed shoppers toward lower-priced options like Costco and Walmart, which makes the re-acceleration more impressive. On margins, Target historically ran a richer operating margin (around 7-8%, near 9% at the pandemic peak) versus Walmart near 6% and Costco around 3.5%, and management is trying to restore those historical levels. He also walks through inventory turnover as a key retail metric: Target's ~8.2 is normal for it but well below Walmart's ~12 and Costco's ~15, reflecting Target's higher-price, lower-turnover strategy.

On valuation, enthusiasm has lifted Target's forward P/E from about 8 to 16.7. He had rated the stock a buy before the run-up but downgraded to Hold about a month ago (August 21) on valuation. His updated DCF pegs fair value at $93 versus a $160 market price, so he sees considerable downside and reiterates Hold, saying the ~3.3% drop since his last review isn't enough to warrant an upgrade — he'd want a bigger pullback or further fundamental improvement first.

Interesting News for Starbucks Stock Investors | SBUX Stock Analysis | BROS Stock
2026-09-27 · 7m
Parkev reacts to Starbucks' announcement that it will close roughly 250 stores across the US and Canada, framing it as further evidence of diseconomies of scale — the company has grown so large (40,000+ locations worldwide) that costs are rising faster than sales. He notes this is the second round of closures and expects more, arguing the chain is over-saturated in North America. He points to a decline in operating margin from about 18% in 2017 to roughly 10% today, and return on invested capital falling from above 30% pre-pandemic to just 11-12%, barely covering the cost of capital.

He attributes much of the margin compression to a structural rise in labor costs: a shortage of frontline restaurant workers (affecting McDonald's and Domino's too) and sharply higher minimum wages, especially California's $20+/hour restaurant wage. In his view these are permanent cost shifts Starbucks didn't anticipate, making incremental new locations unprofitable and justifying a strategic shrinking of the footprint.

Parkev credits CEO Brian Niccol — whose track record at Chipotle earns the stock benefit-of-the-doubt — for identifying and acting on the problem, but warns that giving up market share could open the door for Dutch Bros to expand at Starbucks' expense. On valuation he calls a forward P/E of 30 rich for a company in this position and pegs his DCF fair value at $66 versus a market price of $93. He reiterates a Hold rating, saying he loves the brand but wants a considerably better entry point before upgrading to a buy.

Should Investors Buy Costco Stock Instead of Walmart Stock? | COST Stock Analysis WMT Stock Analysis
2026-09-26 · 7m
Parkev compares Costco and Walmart, two low-cost retail leaders, to decide which is the better buy at current prices. On revenue, Walmart is more than twice the size ($736 billion trailing versus Costco's $294 billion), but he flags that Walmart does this with over 10,000 locations worldwide while Costco generates nearly half that revenue from fewer than 1,000 stores — which he reads as a long runway for Costco to expand. Costco has historically been conservative about adding locations, maximizing revenue per store, but has been slower than Walmart in e-commerce, where Walmart is the clear leader and has even been gaining share against Amazon by using its store network for fast fulfillment.

He spends time on profitability, noting people are often shocked that both companies earn only about $4 of operating profit per $100 of sales — Walmart at 4.4% and Costco at 3.82% (including membership fees). He stresses retail is a structurally low-margin industry. He then focuses on inventory turnover as the metric that matters most for merchants: Costco's ~15.25 is world-class and well above Walmart's ~12, reflecting Costco's discipline in buying and placing inventory.

On valuation, Costco trades at a forward P/E of 36.3, about a 10% premium to Walmart's 34 — rich multiples for brick-and-mortar retailers that reflect investor respect. His DCF work finds Walmart overvalued (fair value $70 versus a $110 price) while Costco looks more fairly valued (fair value $843 versus a $900 price). Given the choice between the two, he would pick Costco today.
