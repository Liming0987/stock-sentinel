Consumer Sentiment is Falling
2026-10-11 · 14m
This is a macro-focused video rather than a single-stock analysis. Parkev unpacks the University of Michigan consumer-sentiment report, which fell to 46.3 in October (down 1.8 points from September's 48.1 and below the 47.3 expectation), near record lows. He frames consumer sentiment as a leading indicator: sour consumers spend less, which slows sales, profits, and hiring, ultimately pressuring stock valuations.

He attributes the gloom primarily to a cumulative, roughly six-year surge in the cost of living since the 2020-2021 COVID stimulus. He distinguishes substitutable from non-substitutable inflation: price rises in goods you can swap away from (e.g., beef for chicken) hurt less, but the bulk of inflation has hit categories consumers cannot avoid — food (all food +2.7%), energy (gasoline +27%, fuel oil +52%, utility gas and electricity up), shelter (+3%), and hospital services (+5.2%). He cites the Iran war and earlier Russia-Ukraine invasion as drivers of elevated oil prices. A rare relief is motor-vehicle insurance, down 5.1%, though vehicle maintenance/repair is up 5.2% and may eventually push insurance back up.

Parkev connects this to equities: weak sentiment should especially hurt discretionary spending (vacations, new cars, theme-park trips like Disney). He references consumer-facing companies he follows — Walmart, Costco, Procter & Gamble, Pepsi, Coca-Cola — all reporting customers trading down to store brands and smaller package sizes, which pressures their profits and hiring.

He balances the bearishness with a key offset: the labor market remains very strong, with unemployment near 4% (near multi-decade lows), and employers are reluctant to fire. He says he will grow concerned only when mass layoffs begin outside AI-driven back-office roles, but for now expects weak sentiment to persist given few catalysts to lower the cost of living. The video closes with a plug for his book on his six-step investing framework.

  • Non-substitutable inflation (food, energy, shelter, healthcare) is driving the bulk of consumer pain
  • Weak sentiment should pressure discretionary categories and the profits of consumer-facing companies
  • A near-4% unemployment rate and reluctant-to-fire employers are the key offset keeping the economy stable

Why Is MongoDB Stock Crashing and is it a Buying Opportunity? | MDB STock Analysis
2026-10-12 · 6m
Parkev examines the recent sell-off in MongoDB after its CEO was poached, forcing an abrupt leadership transition that spooked investors. He frames the disruption as a legitimate concern but notes management simultaneously announced an additional $1 billion share-buyback authorization, which he reads as a signal of insider optimism. The stock is down about 8% year to date despite a rally that preceded the CEO news.

The bulk of the video is a fundamentals review that is strongly positive. Trailing-12-month revenue sits at $2.78 billion, up from roughly $100 million in 2017, and management guided to at least 20% annual revenue growth for the next three years. Margins are improving in lockstep: cash-flow-from-operations-to-sales hit a record 24% (up from -30% in 2019), and R&D as a percentage of sales has fallen from 55% in 2017 to 28%, with room to keep declining as the company scales. Management expects GAAP profitability by the end of 2027. Parkev attributes the strength to organic demand and customers expanding into higher-value add-on services that lift average revenue per user.

On valuation he is more cautious. The forward P/E of 49 is near multi-year lows and, given 20%+ growth and expanding margins, he considers that a fair price. However, his updated discounted-cash-flow model (13.2% WACC) pegs fair value at $293 versus the current $386 price, implying the shares are overvalued on a DCF basis even while appearing fairly valued on forward P/E.

Reconciling the two methods, Parkev reiterates a hold rating rather than upgrading to a buy. He wants to see either continued operational outperformance at a similar price or a pullback closer to his $293 fair-value estimate before calling the dip a buying opportunity.

Revenue Growth is Accelerating at 3M | MMM Stock Analysis
2026-10-11 · 7m
Parkev reviews 3M, the industrial conglomerate, following several years of major restructuring driven partly by rising trade barriers, higher oil prices, and supply shortages. He argues the restructuring is beginning to pay off: organic revenue growth is accelerating and management is guiding to further margin expansion.

A central theme is management's deliberate divestiture of commodity-like businesses — segments where products are undifferentiated and compete only on cost. These divestitures cut total revenue from about $35 billion to roughly $25 billion but improved the quality and organic growth of the remaining portfolio. Parkev uses the grocery-store analogy (nobody cares about the brand of a banana) to explain why commodity businesses are unattractive and why shedding them is sensible.

On profitability, 3M's operating margin has historically hovered around 20% (down from roughly 25% a decade ago), but management is targeting 25% in the near term. The levers include roughly 1.5% portfolio-wide price increases to offset oil costs, reducing manufacturing facilities from about 110 to 100, and using AI to cut overhead in IT, HR, and finance functions.

On valuation, 3M trades at a forward P/E of 16.6, near the high end of its recent range, reflecting investor optimism about the turnaround. His DCF pegs fair value at $143 versus the current $163 price, so the stock looks slightly overvalued on both methods. Parkev concludes it is not yet a buying opportunity and would wait for either a further pullback or more evidence that the restructuring benefits are durable.

Is Planet Labs an Undervalued Space Stock to Buy Right Now in October? | PL Stock Analysis
2026-10-11 · 11m
Parkev dedicates a full deep-dive to Planet Labs (PL), whose stock is down nearly 50% over the prior six months, asking whether the dip is a buying opportunity. The business has turned cash-flow positive and holds nearly a billion dollars in cash, which he treats as a meaningful de-risking inflection.

Fundamentally he is very impressed. Year to date Planet Labs generated roughly $68 million in operating cash flow and $21 million in free cash flow even after higher capex to meet rising demand. It opportunistically raised about $120 million selling stock at an average $32 during the space-stock frenzy (shares now ~$17.50), leaving about $865 million in cash. Remaining performance obligations stood at $753 million (up ~9%), and the Q2 beat came partly from executing a contract earlier than expected. Management says executing existing contracts alone could recognize over $400 million in revenue over the next four quarters. Wall Street analysts project revenue growth of 47% in the fiscal year ending January 2027, 30% the following year, and 28% the year after. Demand is driven by nations securing their regions via satellite imagery, faster/clearer images, and added AI-driven analysis that lifts premium, higher-margin revenue. Operating margin is improving from -125% to -25%.

Where Parkev hesitates is valuation. The stock trades at a forward price-to-sales of 11 — roughly where it sat before the frenzy, having peaked above 30 during the SpaceX-IPO-driven euphoria. He draws extensive parallels to past frenzies he warned against (meme stocks 2020-21, EV stocks, quantum computing), arguing he instinctively avoids herd behavior and applies a higher threshold to such names.

His DCF pegs fair value at $5.50 versus the $17.50 market price. He concludes Planet Labs is an exciting, well-run, robustly growing business he would like to own — but not at these levels. He would want roughly another 50% drop to around $8 before meaningfully considering a purchase, and would not buy near current prices all else equal.

Domino's Analysis: Value Stock or Value Trap? | DPZ Stock Analysis
2026-10-11 · 7m
Parkev evaluates Domino's Pizza, asking whether it is a value stock or a value trap. He opens with positive developments: Domino's has secured the top position on both Uber Eats and DoorDash, and management reports customers ordering more frequently than ever. The offsetting concern is that average order value is falling — people order more often but spend less per order — which is management's near-term focus.

Much of the video celebrates Domino's long-term business quality. On valuation, Domino's trades at a forward P/E of 14.72, near its cheapest in years. Parkev attributes the depressed multiple to macro headwinds: rising global interest rates increasing the opportunity cost of capital and pulling money toward bonds, plus falling consumer disposable income amid a higher cost of living.

His updated DCF values the business at $326, about 6% above the current market price, making the stock look slightly undervalued. He concludes that Domino's is a buying opportunity at current prices, calling it one of the better businesses he covers and saying he likes the risk-versus-reward here.

Parkev explains the ongoing trouble at Nike, whose stock keeps making new 52-week lows and is down over 46% in 2026. Management forecast a difficult current fiscal year and hinted the pain could extend into the next, so this video aims to clarify what is happening. He stresses the declining, year-over-year revenue is not a surprise — management expected it and said Q1 results were in line with their own guidance.

The weakness is concentrated in Nike Sportswear, the Jordan brand, and Greater China, while the Nike performance business is holding up. The most concerning issue is inventory: Nike is struggling to move aged, higher-volume sportswear footwear even after price cuts, discounts, and promotions, and that excess clogs wholesale partners' shelves and suppresses new orders. Parkev links this to a cautious consumer trading down to cheaper goods — a theme he hears from nearly every consumer-facing company he follows (Lululemon, Costco, Walmart, Target, Procter & Gamble, Clorox, Kimberly-Clark) — as shrinking disposable income and higher living costs squeeze discretionary spending.

Parkev notes one bright spot: where Nike delivers genuinely new products, it sees early success, and he argues the company has leaned too long on legacy franchises (Air Force 1s, Jordans) in new colorways rather than true innovation.

He then pushes back on Nike's plan to restore the scarcity model for Jordan retro launches by deliberately cutting their volume and frequency. He sees Jordan as Nike's best IP and highest-priced product (recent retros around $215), and worries that Jordan's cultural relevance is fading with younger generations, so reducing supply of a declining-relevance product seems backward. He concedes, though, that his prior judgment of Nike's direct-to-consumer push was wrong, so this scarcity strategy might still work out. Overall he is unenthusiastic about management's turnaround approach.

Best Semiconductor Stocks to Buy in October: Marvell vs. Qualcomm
2026-10-11 · 6m
Parkev compares two asset-light semiconductor companies, Marvell (MRVL) and Qualcomm (QCOM), to decide which is the better buy. He frames Marvell as already benefiting from the AI spending boom, while Qualcomm is on the cusp of capturing AI-driven data-center revenue but currently feels only the negative side effects.

On revenue, Qualcomm is the larger business at $44 billion TTM (up from roughly $25 billion a decade ago, less than a doubling), while Marvell has grown far faster to $9.45 billion TTM from under $1 billion ten years ago. Qualcomm's core smartphone segment is struggling as higher component prices weigh on handset purchases, but it has signed data-center semiconductor agreements with Meta Platforms and is in talks with others including Amazon — revenue Parkev expects to materialize in mid-to-late 2027 rather than 2026. He notes Qualcomm has successfully diversified before, into automotive and PCs.

On profitability, Qualcomm leads on absolute operating margin (23.5%) and ROIC (22.9%, up from 12% in 2017), while Marvell leads on improvement — operating margin swinging from -10% in 2025 to 16.8% TTM, and ROIC up to 12.6% from about 4% in 2017.

On valuation, Marvell trades at a forward P/E of 42.5, nearly three times Qualcomm's 14, reflecting its current AI exposure. Parkev's DCF values Marvell at $200 (vs. $287 price), so it looks overvalued on both methods, while Qualcomm's DCF fair value is $253 (vs. $181 price), making it undervalued on both. He concludes Qualcomm is the clear winner — better margins and ROIC, cheaper valuation, with a 2027 AI catalyst ahead — and discloses he already owns Qualcomm and has no interest in Marvell near current prices.
